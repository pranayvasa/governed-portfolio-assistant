As a self-study exercise, I asked a language model 20 questions about a sample lending policy. On one of them, it quoted a clause number that does not exist, with a confident explanation of what the clause required.

The model wasn't broken. It was doing what it does: producing the most plausible answer. A plausible clause number is still a fabricated one.

Two things changed how I test since then. First, every question set now includes questions the policy deliberately doesn't answer, so I can see whether the model says "not covered" or invents something. Second, I check every citation against the source, not just the ones that look wrong.

In lending, a made-up policy clause in a decision is a model risk event, not a typo.
